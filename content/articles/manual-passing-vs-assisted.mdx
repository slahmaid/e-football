Assisted passing is the default for a reason: it reduces mistakes when you are learning patterns. Manual passing is the upgrade when those patterns are already automatic — and when assisted starts choosing the wrong teammate under pressure.

## What assisted actually does

Assisted helps with:

- Weight of pass on open lanes
- Selecting the “obvious” receiver
- Reducing underhit balls to feet

It struggles when:

- Two teammates overlap in the same channel
- You want a disguised reverse ball
- The AI prefers a safe outlet you do not want

## When to stay assisted

Stay assisted if you are still building muscle memory for a new formation, or if your loss streak comes from **decision speed**, not pass accuracy. Switching to manual while tilted usually makes things worse.

## When to go manual

Flip to manual when:

1. You already know who should receive the ball before you press pass.
2. Opponents are reading your assisted patterns and stepping into lanes.
3. You need lofted or driven passes that assisted softens too much.

A practical rule: **if you can name the receiver out loud before the animation starts, you are ready for manual.**

## Hybrid approach

Many top players keep shooting assisted and change only pass assistance. That keeps finishing stable while giving you full control of circulation. Try it for ten matches before judging.

## Training drill

In training, play five minutes of exclusive one-touch manual passing between midfielders. No shots. When your error rate drops under one bad pass per minute, take it online.

Manual is not a personality flex. It is a tool — use it when assisted starts playing someone else’s pattern for you.
