Konami’s official **v6.0.0** season update rebuilt Game Plans around possession. With **Fluid Formation** enabled, your team can hold one shape when attacking and another when defending — exactly the split many managers already think in, now supported in-engine.

## What Konami added

According to the [official Version Info for v6.0.0](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en/page/v6/versioninfo_v6-00), Fluid Formation is a Game Plan option. Once toggled on, you configure:

- an **attacking formation**
- a **defensive formation**

Auto-pick players also expands so both shapes can be filled automatically when Fluid Formation is active.

## How it behaves in a match

When you have the ball, the game moves players toward the attacking setup. When you lose it, the defensive setup takes over. That means a full-back can push higher in possession, or a wide attacker can drop into a more defensive lane out of possession — ideas Konami highlighted in both the Version Info and the [pre-season news post](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en/topic/news/5558).

## Practical setups that make sense

| Attack shape | Defence shape | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4-2-3-1 | 4-4-2 flat | Stable Division play |
| 3-2-4-1 / 4-3-3 | 5-3-2 | Controlling the ball, then shutting counters |
| 4-1-4-1 | 4-5-1 | Mid-block grinders |

Keep player roles readable. If your attacking AMF becomes a second CB out of possession, recovery runs get messy.

## PixelPitch tip

Turn Fluid Formation **on**, build both shapes, then play five matches tracking only transition goals conceded. If you leak goals in the first five seconds after turnovers, your defensive shape is too high or your midfield instructions are too aggressive.
